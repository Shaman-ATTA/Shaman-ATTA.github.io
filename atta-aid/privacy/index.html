ATTA Aid — Privacy Policy

Effective date: 16 November 2026 · Last updated: 17 November 2026 (app version 0.5.7) · App: ATTA Aid (Android, package com.attacorp.aid) · Developer: ATTA CORP (Google Play developer “ATTA”) · Contact: gamerazrabplay@gmail.com

Short version

  • ATTA Aid has no ads, no accounts, no servers, no analytics and no advertising ID. We (the developer) do not receive any of your data unless you choose to e-mail us.
  • Guides, tools, progress, settings and your age group stay on your phone.
  • The optional offline chat sends your nickname, a random device ID, your messages and — only if you choose — your SOS GPS location directly to phones nearby over Bluetooth / Wi-Fi. There are no servers. Phones nearby can relay messages (up to 5 hops). Direct messages are end-to-end encrypted (only the recipient can read them), group messages are encrypted with the group key, and the “Everyone nearby” channel and SOS are intentionally not encrypted, so everyone nearby can read them.
  • ATTA Aid is intended for users 13 and older and is not directed at children under 13. If a younger person uses it anyway (for example on a supervised school trip), a built-in child mode limits chat to a group created by an adult, after a parent or teacher turns chat on.
  • New in 0.5.0: you can share your location in a chat or SOS, and turn on a group radar — always by your own choice, encrypted in groups and direct chats. An optional medical card stays encrypted on your phone. The offline map downloads map tiles once from OpenFreeMap (the tile server sees your IP address, like any website); nothing else goes online.
  • New in 0.5.7: optional emergency SMS contacts stay only on your phone and are excluded from backup. If you tick “Send SMS too” on an SOS, the app opens your system SMS app with a prefilled message; you press Send. The app does not use the SEND_SMS or READ_CONTACTS permissions. Nearby phones can send an SOS delivery receipt (“Delivered: …”) and an “I'm coming” reply; a group can run a check-in (“I'm OK” / “I need help”). An opt-in lock-screen medical card (off by default), a 6-month go-bag reminder, and the home-screen SOS widget and Quick Settings tile stay on the device; the widget and tile only open the confirmation countdown and never send an SOS by themselves.

2. Offline chat (nearby phones)

When you turn the chat on, the app uses Google Nearby Connections (part of Google Play services) to connect directly to other phones nearby that run ATTA Aid — without the internet. It sends:

  • your nickname and a random device ID created by the app (not your phone number, name or Google account). For users under 13 the nickname is not broadcast to strangers — it is sent only inside the encrypted group;
  • your phone's public chat key: on first start each phone creates a random key pair for end-to-end encryption. The private key never leaves the phone; the public key is broadcast to nearby phones so they can send you encrypted direct messages. It is not linked to your name, phone number or Google account;
  • the text of your messages, their time, and message type;
  • for an SOS, your GPS coordinates and accuracy — only if you leave “Include my location” on (off by default for users under 13). Before sending an SOS you choose who gets it: everyone nearby, your group, or one person.

Who can see it:

  • Everyone nearby (13+ only) and SOS: these are intentionally not encrypted, so that everyone nearby can read them quickly. Anyone nearby running the app can see them, and other phones can relay them up to 5 hops. Do not post secrets there.
  • Group messages are encrypted (AES-GCM) with a key shared only through the group's QR / code. Relaying phones forward them but cannot read them. Anyone who has the group code can read group messages, so share it only with the group.
  • Direct messages are end-to-end encrypted (X25519 key agreement + AES-256-GCM): only the recipient's phone can read them; your nickname and text travel only inside the encrypted message. Relaying phones forward them but cannot read or change them; they can only see the sender and recipient device IDs and the time. A direct message is sent only once the recipient's public key is known. In each direct chat you can compare a safety code in person to check that nobody swapped the keys.

SOS receipts, “I'm coming” and group check-in: when someone receives your SOS, their phone can send a short delivery receipt (“Delivered: …”) and they can tap “I'm coming”. In a group, members can mark “I'm OK” or “I need help”, and the group owner can ask everyone to check in. These are ordinary mesh messages: they use the same encryption as the channel they travel on (the group key, end-to-end encryption for a direct SOS, or none on the open channel / an unencrypted SOS), contain a nickname and an SOS id, and go only to nearby phones.

We cannot delete messages from other people's phones, because there is no server. Google Play services processes the radio connection; see the Google Privacy Policy.

Technical note: the Bluetooth / Wi-Fi link between two phones is encrypted by Nearby Connections. According to Google, when apps use the Nearby SDK, Google Play services collects usage analytics (discovery and connection latency, reliability and throughput, together with device model, country, build version and the app package name); you can allow or deny this in Settings → Google → Usage & diagnostics (source). This is done by Google Play services, not by ATTA Aid, and does not include messages.

3. Children

ATTA Aid is intended for users 13 and older and is not directed at children under 13. Because it may still be used by younger people under adult supervision (for example on a school trip), it includes a protective child mode. At first launch the app shows a neutral age question (birth year, or “prefer not to say”). Only the resulting age group is stored, on the phone. If no age is given, the app treats the user as a child.

For users under 13:

  • the open “Everyone nearby” chat and messages to strangers are off; they can chat only in a group created by a teacher or parent (13+) and in direct messages with members of that group;
  • chat stays off until an adult confirms with their birth year and sets a parent PIN in Settings → Parental controls; the adult can turn chat off again at any time;
  • a safety reminder is shown before the first message;
  • their nickname is shared only inside the encrypted group;
  • SOS remains available to everyone for safety; SOS location is off by default and is only sent if the child chooses it;
  • the group owner (teacher) can see the member list and remove members, which creates a new group key.

The app has no ads, no analytics SDK and no advertising ID, and we do not collect any personal information from children on our servers (we have none). Only nearby phones receive what is described in section 2. Parents can delete everything by clearing app data or uninstalling. We designed this mode with the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), the EU/UK GDPR rules for children (including Art. 8) and the Israeli Protection of Privacy Law in mind. If you believe a child sent us personal data by e-mail, contact us and we will delete it.

4. Location

  • My coordinates tool and the map: your position is shown on screen only and is not stored or sent.
  • SOS: your GPS coordinates, their accuracy and the time they were measured are added only while “Include my GPS location” is on (on by default for 13+, off by default in child mode). The SOS goes to the recipients you pick; “Everyone nearby” is not encrypted.
  • “Share my location” in a chat: only when you tap the button. In a group or a direct chat the coordinates travel inside the encryption. In the open “Everyone nearby” channel they are readable by everyone nearby, so the app asks you to confirm first; this is not available in child mode.
  • Group radar (“Where is my group”): only if you turn it on. While it is on, your position is sent about every 45 seconds (every 2 minutes with battery saver) only to members of your group, encrypted with the group key, and a notification shows that sharing is on; tap Stop at any time. To keep working with the screen off, the app runs a foreground service of type “location”; it uses only the “while using the app” location permission (no background-location permission). Children can use the radar only inside the group created by their teacher or parent. Members' positions are kept in memory on the receiving phones and forgotten after an hour; the group owner gets a warning if a member is farther than the chosen distance or has not been seen for 5 minutes.
  • Offline map: when you tap “Download area around me”, the app downloads map tiles for a limited area (at most 3,000 tiles or 50 MB) from OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org, served via the Cloudflare network). Like any website, the server receives your IP address, the requested map tiles (which reveal the approximate area) and the app's name/version (User-Agent). No account, key or identifier of yours is sent. See the OpenFreeMap Terms of Service. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL). Viewing the map later works offline.
  • On Android 11 and older, Android requires the location permission to scan for nearby Bluetooth devices; the app does not use it to track you.

5. Reports and blocking

You can report a message or block a person (long-press a message). Reports and blocks are stored on your phone only; the reported message is hidden for you. If you choose “send by e-mail”, your e-mail app sends us the report, including the reported nickname, device ID and message text, plus your e-mail address. We use reports only to improve safety in the app, keep them for up to 12 months and then delete them.

6. Support the project and feedback

There are no ads and no paid features. In Settings → Support the project you can rate the app on Google Play, share a link, or e-mail us feedback (your e-mail app sends us your message and address; we use it only to reply and delete it within 12 months). If we enable optional tips (voluntary in-app donations) in a future version, payment is handled entirely by Google Play Billing; we receive only an order confirmation, not your card details. Tips are hidden for users under 13 and unlock nothing.

7. Permissions

Bluetooth and Nearby devices / Wi-Fi (offline chat) · Location (coordinates, SOS, Bluetooth scan on old Android) · Notifications (new messages, background relay) · Camera is not requested (group QR codes are opened with your phone's own camera app) · Wake lock (keep the screen on for SOS signal). All permissions are optional; the guides work without them. Foreground service “location” is used only while you share your position with the group radar. The app does not request the SEND_SMS or READ_CONTACTS permissions. If you add emergency contacts, you can type them or pick them with the system contact picker (which grants a one-time read of the chosen contact only). If you tick “Send SMS too” on an SOS, the app opens your system SMS app with a prefilled message; you decide whether to send it. The home-screen SOS widget and the Quick Settings tile only open the SOS confirmation and countdown; they never send an alert by themselves.

8. Legal bases (GDPR / UK GDPR)

Running the chat you turn on — performance of the service you request and our legitimate interest in providing it; sharing SOS location — your consent (or, for a child, the vital interests of the person in danger), which you can withdraw at any time; enabling chat for a child under 13 — consent of the parent or teacher given in the app; handling e-mailed reports and feedback — legitimate interest in keeping the community safe and answering you.

9. Your rights

Because we hold no data on servers, most data is only on your device, where you can view and delete it yourself. For anything you sent us by e-mail you can contact gamerazrabplay@gmail.com:

  • EU/EEA/UK (GDPR): access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection, portability, withdrawal of consent, and a complaint to your data protection authority.
  • Israel (Protection of Privacy Law, 5741-1981): the right to review and correct information about you; complaints to the Privacy Protection Authority.
  • USA (COPPA): parents can ask us to review or delete any information their child sent us and refuse further collection.
  • California (CCPA/CPRA): the right to know, delete and correct. We do not sell or share personal information and show no ads. We will not discriminate against you for exercising your rights.

10. Security and retention

Data on your phone is protected by Android app sandboxing; your private chat key is additionally protected by the Android Keystore. Direct messages are end-to-end encrypted (X25519 + AES-256-GCM); group messages are encrypted with AES-GCM using the group key; the “Everyone nearby” channel and SOS are intentionally not encrypted. On-device data is kept until you delete it; e-mailed reports and feedback up to 12 months.

11. Health information

ATTA Aid is an educational guide and not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition. The optional medical card is information you type in; it is stored only on your phone, encrypted, is never sent to us, and leaves the phone only as a short summary inside an SOS if you switch that on (off by default). Always seek professional medical help. You can optionally turn on a lock-screen medical card (off by default, with an in-app privacy warning). If you do, a low-priority public notification can show a short ICE summary (name, blood type, allergies, emergency contact) on the lock screen so a rescuer can read it without unlocking the phone. The medical-card file itself remains encrypted and excluded from backup.
